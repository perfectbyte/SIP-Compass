Your monthly ritual

  • 01
    Open the SIP in the "Direct – Growth" plan on any SEBI-registered app or the fund house's own site. Direct plans skip the 0.5–1% yearly commission. Use a date just after salary day.
  • 02
    Never stop when markets fall. A falling market is when your SIP buys the most units. The Time Machine shows what stopping in a crash really costs.
  • 03
    Got a bonus? Add it to the same funds. If a large lump sum makes you nervous, park it in a liquid fund and move it in over 6–12 months with an STP. Don't wait for a "dip"; nobody times it reliably.
  • 04
    Raise the SIP every year when your salary goes up. A 10% step-up roughly doubles your final amount over 20 years.
  • 05
    Look once a year, not daily. Only switch a fund for the reasons on The Rules page. Never because another fund did better last year.
Goal Guard · protect what a goal needs

Don't let a crash land in year 7 of a 10-year goal

Enter a goal and what you already hold for it. Goal Guard counts down to the date, shows how much should already be out of equity, what to move, and whether you're short. It protects the amount the goal needs, not your whole portfolio. These are planning numbers at the assumptions below, not guarantees.

New here? How Goal Guard works, in four steps
  1. The cost on the date. Prices rise every year, so ₹25 L of fees today costs more in 2030. We grow your amount by inflation (6% a year unless you change it).
  2. Two kinds of money. Equity funds grow faster but can fall 30–50% in a bad year. Safe money means debt funds, liquid funds or FDs: it grows slowly but doesn't crash. Far from the date, growth matters most. Close to the date, a crash could ruin the goal with no time to recover.
  3. The shift. From 5 years before the date, the plan moves the goal's money step by step from equity into safe money, so that by 1 year before, the whole amount is safe. Your monthly SIP is used first; equity is sold only for what the SIP can't cover, spread over up to 12 months.
  4. Three futures. We check the plan if markets do OK, if they are slow, and if they are slow and crash tomorrow. If even the slow case falls short, we show what would close the gap: a bigger SIP, a later date or a smaller goal.

You can change every assumption under “Assumptions you can change”. Nothing you type leaves this computer.

Debt, decoded

Seven things nobody tells you

01 · Not an FD

The value moves every day

An FD promises a rate, and bank deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per bank. A debt fund has no such insurance and passes on whatever its bonds earn, including losses. In return you get no lock-in, tax only when you sell, and usually a slightly better rate.

02 · The seesaw

Rates up, bond prices down

When RBI raises rates, new bonds pay more, so old bonds fall in price. A fund holding 10-year bonds can drop 5–8% when rates jump. Short-term funds barely notice. Match the fund's term to when you need the money.

03 · Credit accidents

It has happened before

IL&FS (2018), DHFL (June 2019), Yes Bank (March 2020) and Franklin Templeton shutting six schemes (April 2020) all hit "safe" debt funds overnight. Our detector flags any fund that fell sharply in a single day.

04 · The yield trap

Extra return means extra risk

If one fund pays 1% more than its peers, it is usually lending to weaker companies. We flag those funds and cut their score. Credit risk funds are shown for completeness, but never recommended to beginners.

05 · Tax since April 2023

Same slab as FD, paid later

Debt fund gains are taxed at your slab rate, like FD interest, with no indexation. The difference: FD interest is taxed every year (and TDS is cut), while a fund is taxed only when you withdraw, so your money compounds untaxed meanwhile.

06 · High-slab trick

Arbitrage is taxed like equity

Arbitrage funds earn FD-like returns from price gaps, but tax is 20% (under 1 year) or 12.5% (after 1 year). For anyone in the 30% slab that is a big saving. Income + Arbitrage FoFs get 12.5% after 2 years.

07 · Emergency money

Liquid fund, never a long bond

Liquid funds let you withdraw up to ₹50,000 instantly and the rest the next working day. Their value almost never falls. Keep 6 months of expenses here before you start any equity SIP.

Why many SIPs fall short of expectations

Money leaks · two quiet costs most SIP investors miss

Neither shows up as a loss on your statement. Both can cost more than a bad fund choice.

Leak 1 · commission

Regular plan or Direct plan?

The same fund comes in two versions. A Regular plan, sold through a bank or an agent, pays them a yearly commission from your money. A Direct plan, bought from the fund house or a direct-plan app, doesn't. Your statement or app shows which one you hold: look for “Regular” or “Direct” in the fund's name.

Leak 2 · tax

Use your ₹1.25 lakh tax-free gain every year

Gains on equity funds held over a year are tax-free up to ₹1.25 lakh in each financial year (April–March). Unused room doesn't carry forward. Booking gains up to that limit, then reinvesting straight away, raises your cost price, so less of the gain is taxed when you finally sell.

The whole playbook

Ten rules that beat the FD

01 · Start with you

Time decides the fund

Need it in under 3 years: no equity at all. 3–5 years: mostly debt. 8+ years: mostly equity. Pick the category first, the fund second.

02 · Ignore the leaderboard

Last year's winner rarely repeats

Small-cap toppers of one year often sink to the bottom half within two. Chasing them is the number one reason SIP returns end up below FD rates.

03 · Rolling returns

Judge every window, not one

We check every 3-year stretch (starting each week) over the last 5 years. The typical and worst stretch tell you what an ordinary investor really got.

04 · Crash behaviour

Falling less matters more

Down capture below 100 means the fund falls less than Nifty 50 when markets drop. A fund that falls 25% instead of 35% needs far less to recover.

05 · Large caps

Just buy the index

Most active large-cap funds fail to beat Nifty 50 after costs, because they all must hold the same top 100 companies. A cheap index fund is the smart default.

06 · Mid and small

Where fund managers earn their fee

Active funds have more room to win here. But small caps can fall 50–60% and take years to recover. Keep them to a slice, only for 10+ year money.

07 · Balance

Keep 20% out of equity

Debt and gold cushion crashes and give you cash to buy more when equity is down 30%. Gold has usually risen or held when stocks fell.

08 · Fewer is better

3 to 5 funds, never 12

Two large-cap funds own the same stocks. More funds means more overlap, not more safety.

09 · Safety funds

High return is a red flag

In liquid and bond funds, a return well above peers usually means credit risk. We score these on steadiness and flag unusually high yields.

10 · When to exit

Switch for reasons, not results

Exit if the fund manager changes and the style changes with them, the fund drifts from its label, or it lags peers and its index for 3+ years. Not for one bad year.

How the score works

A one-day NAV jump that snaps straight back is deleted only when it is larger than 8% for equity, index and gold, or larger than 2% for debt. Smaller crash-and-bounce days stay. A face-value or split change is rescaled in a separate step. Debt funds lose 15 points for a sharp one-day fall in the last 10 years (so events such as DHFL in June 2019 still count), 10 points for a side-pocket, and 8 points for a yield well above peers. Each measure is compared with the other funds in the same official AMFI category, cut off on one shared NAV date, then turned into a 0–100 score. 50 means category average. Index and gold scores are a tie-break at best: compare Direct-plan costs. Liquid and arbitrage remain ranked; small differences deserve little weight. Compare costs and, for liquid funds, current credit quality on the factsheet. Data: AMFI daily NAVs via the free api.mfapi.in, refreshed on your first visit each day.

About SIP Compass · what it is, what it isn't, where your data goes
What it is

A free, open-source learning tool for Indian mutual fund investors who want to understand and manage their own SIPs. It runs from a folder on your computer: no account, no login, no sign-up.

What it isn't

It is not investment advice, and its author (Perfectbyte) is not a SEBI-registered investment adviser or research analyst. Rankings, flags and plans are calculations from public data under the assumptions shown. Check the scheme documents, and an adviser if you need one, before you act.

Your data

Everything you type (goals, holdings, plans) is saved only in this browser on this computer. Nothing is uploaded and there is no tracking. Clearing your browser's site data erases it.

Use this to move your goals and plan to another browser, folder or computer, or to keep a backup. The file stays wherever you save it.

What the page fetches

Public fund data only: NAV histories from mfapi.in (which republishes AMFI data), and the page fonts from Google Fonts. The optional local updater adds expense ratios and fund sizes from AMFI. Figures are before tax and costs.
